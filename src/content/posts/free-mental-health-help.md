## If you need support right now (very important)

Before anything else, these 3 are free, confidential, and available immediately. No appointment, no insurance, no cost.

**988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline:** Call or text 988, or chat online at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org). Available 24/7/365, in more than 150 languages, connecting you to a trained crisis counselor through a nationwide network of local crisis centers.

**Crisis Text Line:** Text **HOME to 741741**. Free, 24/7, confidential, entirely text-based, built specifically for people who find typing easier than talking in the moment.

**SAMHSA's National Helpline:** Call **1-800-662-HELP (4357)**, or text **HELP4U** to the same number. Free, confidential, 24/7/365, available in English and Spanish. This isn't a crisis line specifically, it's a treatment referral and information service that connects you to local treatment facilities, support groups, and community organizations. Learn more at [samhsa.gov/find-help/national-helpline](https://www.samhsa.gov/find-help/helplines/national-helpline).

**One honest heads-up worth knowing:** 988 was actually proposed for elimination earlier in the 2026 federal budget process. It survived, and the FY2026 Cooperative Agreement confirms **$231,482,876** in active funding right now. I'm telling you this the same way I told you about LIHEAP and community health center funding in earlier posts, it's real and funded today, but not something to assume is permanently guaranteed. Use it. Don't assume it'll always be there in exactly this form.

## 1. The nonprofit network most people have never heard of

**Open Path Collective:** this is the standout find of this entire post, and it's exactly the kind of genuinely underused resource this series keeps uncovering.

It's a nonprofit network of over **40,000 licensed therapists** who've committed to charging members **$40 to $70 per session**, with student-intern sessions running as low as **$30**. There's a **one-time $65 lifetime membership fee**, and that's it, no recurring membership cost on top of session prices.

**Eligibility:** your household income needs to be under **$100,000**, and your current insurance needs to be inadequate or unaffordable for regular therapy. This isn't crisis support. It's real, ongoing therapy with a licensed professional, at a price most people assume simply doesn't exist without insurance.

Apply directly at [openpathcollective.org](https://openpathcollective.org).

## 2. Sliding-scale and community options

**Community mental health centers and Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs):** they offer mental health services on the same sliding-scale structure I covered in [Free and Low-Cost Clinics Near You](/blog/free-sliding-scale-healthcare-dental), often running **free to around $30 per visit** based on your income. If you've already found your nearest FQHC through that post, call and ask specifically about their mental health services, many offer this alongside medical and dental care under the same roof.

**University training clinics:** they are a genuinely underused option almost nobody thinks to search for. Graduate students in psychology or counseling programs provide therapy under direct, licensed faculty supervision, at significantly reduced rates, often a fraction of what a private practice charges. Search **"[your nearest university] psychology training clinic"** or **"counseling training clinic"** to find one near you. The care is real and supervised, it's simply delivered as part of a student's clinical training.

## 3. The free benefit already sitting inside your job

**Employee Assistance Programs (EAPs):** they are one of the most overlooked resources on this entire list, and the irony is that a huge number of readers already have access to one right now and don't know it.

Most EAPs offer a set number of **completely free counseling sessions per year**, fully confidential from your employer, HR never sees what you discuss. Check your employee benefits portal, or simply ask HR directly, "do we have an EAP, and how do I access it?" This costs you nothing to ask, and the answer is yes far more often than people assume.

## 4. Free peer support

**NAMI's HelpLine:** offers free peer support, staffed Monday through Friday, and it's run by the same organization behind the holiday-stress statistic that opened this post. Peer support isn't a replacement for therapy, but it's a real, free, judgment-free place to talk to someone who understands what you're navigating, particularly useful for the specific weight the holiday season brings. Reach them through [nami.org](https://www.nami.org).

## How to actually choose where to start

If you're in crisis right now, start with 988 or Crisis Text Line, before anything else on this list. If you're looking for ongoing support and you're not in crisis, check your EAP first, since it's genuinely free and requires zero paperwork beyond asking. If you don't have one or you've used up your sessions, Open Path Collective is the next strongest option, since the cost is transparent and the therapist network is large enough that you have real choice in who you work with.
