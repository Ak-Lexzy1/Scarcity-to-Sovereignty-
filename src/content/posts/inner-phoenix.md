We lost my eldest sibling when I was still young. I didn't have the words for what that kind of loss does to a family back then, but I remember watching it happen to my mother in real time. She drifted through our house like someone had switched her off. Not gone, but not fully there either. I noticed glimpses from my dad too, but it's vague in my memory as I write, because he wasn't always around, as he worked round the clock.

That grief mixed with something else in our house during that period. It's the real financial strain we pass through often. It wasn't one or the other for us. It was both at once, stacked on top of each other, and I didn't have the language for either.

Her sadness rubbed off on me before I even understood what was happening. I pulled away from people. I stopped wanting to be around anyone. I don't think I even realized I was becoming a different version of myself, quieter, more closed off, until a summer trip to my grandparents' house pulled me out of it, mostly by accident.

That was the first time I understood something I'd spend years relearning as an adult, in different forms and different seasons of my life. The time I lost a loved one, the times I was facing financial hardship, lost money to businesses, even when I got heartbroken as a fresh college student, to mention but a few.

That spark you once had feels gone. Your confidence is fading. Self-doubt and fear slowly move in to take its place. You start wishing you could go back to the days you felt unstoppable, back before whatever happened, happened.

When you don't feel like yourself anymore, it's not the end. It's a signal.

During my period of financial struggles, there were days I would stay on the phone with my mom. One of her statements that keeps ringing in my ears was, "You can't turn back time, but you can turn yourself around." I knew this was coming from all the challenges life had thrown at her, which she had endured and miraculously survived.

And that's true. You may not be able to rewind life, but you can rebuild how you see yourself, and that changes everything, because your life always follows the direction of your dominant thoughts.

Your mind is your steering wheel. This isn't cliché motivation. It's reality. What you think consistently becomes the path you actually walk. So here's a small challenge that tends to create disproportionately big shifts. I call it "the money-mind challenge."

I mentioned "money" on purpose, because a larger percentage of the challenges people have can be solved with money. I've been there before, and I can tell you that. Don't get me wrong. Money may not solve all problems, but give $5 million to an American, and most of their problems will disappear overnight. You get the picture now, right?

Back to the money-mind challenge.

Grab a piece of paper. Write down the biggest realistic amount of money you need to make, based on whatever timeframe you're working with. Look at it daily. Say it out loud: "I will make this money." Tell one or two people you trust with your life, if you want to.

Your mind will genuinely surprise you here, because it starts showing you paths you'd never noticed before. Paths that make the number feel possible instead of imaginary.

Here is a warning you must take really seriously. Guard your mind like your life depends on it.

Why does this matter so much? Because two forces shape your future: what you tell yourself, and what other people tell you.

Cut off anyone who makes you doubt your own potential. For the people you genuinely can't remove, like family, certain siblings, coworkers you're stuck with, you don't have to fight them. You just have to stop absorbing what they say as truth.

I remember one such person I had to do this to was one of my siblings. They couldn't see the logic of some decisions I was taking when I'd had enough with financial struggles. In truth, they were watching out for me, but it wasn't helping. Being family, I couldn't cut them off completely, but I'm glad we're now good and back as great buddies. If that had been a friend, they might have been gone for good.

Please don't get me wrong. I'm not saying you should cut off every person in your life simply because you're on a transformation journey. You can simply take a break if they're really going to stop your evolution, and get back once the coast is clear and you've attained your goals. But that's only if they won't drag you back to where you used to be.

When the enemy attacks, it attacks your mind first, meaning your family, friends, or acquaintances may not even be your problem in some cases.

If the negative voice is coming from inside you, argue with it. Disagree with it out loud if you have to. Remind yourself of the times you actually won, and the storms you've already survived and are still standing after.

Why? Because your thoughts are prophecy.

As I said earlier, your life will always move in the direction of your most dominant thoughts. Fix your mind, and watch your future quietly begin fixing itself. It won't come easy. But it is possible, and I'm proof of that, several times over now.

Don't forget. Write the number you want to earn, repeat it daily, guard your mind fiercely, and watch how life quietly starts rearranging itself to match what you actually expect from it.

Your rebirth begins in your thoughts. Protect them with everything you've got, because they're your inner phoenix.
