So here's my take on moneymaxxing, from a different angle. It still includes the good spending and saving advice you already know, but the bigger moves come first.

## What Is Moneymaxxing (And Is It Just Another Trend)?

Moneymaxxing means squeezing more value out of the money you already earn. It borrows its name from looksmaxxing and sleepmaxxing, the internet's habit of turning self-improvement into a sport.

Is it new? No. Cashback, rewards points, savings interest and smart debt payoff have been around forever. A trend with a catchy name just got people looking at their money again. I'll take that.

My opinion: moneymaxxing works only if you start with the big levers. Saving $4 on coffee while your savings account pays you ten cents a year is rearranging chairs. So we're going big first.

## 10 Moneymaxxing Ways to Make Your Money Work Harder

Skip any that don't fit your life. Nobody needs all ten, and the quick guide after the list will point you to yours.

### 1. Fix Where Your Idle Cash Sleeps (High-Yield Savings, Treasury Bills and the Brokerage Trap)

**What it really is:** Every dollar you aren't spending is sleeping somewhere. Where it sleeps decides whether it earns or just lies there. Standard savings accounts at the biggest banks often pay a rounding error. Cash waiting inside a brokerage account can be worse. Many default to a "sweep" account that pays close to nothing while the firm earns real interest on your money.

**How to squeeze it:** Give your cash three jobs. Spending money stays in checking. Your emergency fund goes into a high-yield savings account at an FDIC-insured online bank. Money you won't touch for a few months can go into Treasury bills at [https://www.treasurydirect.gov](https://www.treasurydirect.gov). Their interest skips state income tax, which matters if you live in a high-tax state. Then log into any brokerage account and ask one question: where does my uninvested cash actually go?

**Why bother:** $1,000 at a big bank's standard rate earns roughly ten cents a year. At a competitive online rate, it's around forty dollars. Same money, same you, zero extra effort.

| Where the cash sits | What it usually pays | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard big-bank savings | Close to nothing | Nothing, honestly |
| Brokerage sweep account | Often close to nothing | Short pauses between trades |
| High-yield savings account | Competitive, but rates move | Emergency fund |
| Treasury bills | Similar, state-tax exempt | Cash you can park for weeks or months |

**Do:** Check your rate every few months, because online banks change them.

**Don't:** Chase a flashy teaser rate without reading the conditions. Some require direct deposits or expire after six months. And don't tie up your true emergency money in anything you can't reach quickly.

### 2. Turn a Health Account Into a Stealth Retirement Fund (HSA)

**What it really is:** A health savings account is a retirement account wearing a medical costume. Money goes in untaxed, grows untaxed, and comes out untaxed for medical costs. No other account does all three. You need a qualifying high-deductible plan to open one, and as of 2026, bronze and catastrophic marketplace plans now qualify too. Most people have no idea.

**How to squeeze it:** The power move: pay your current medical bills from regular cash, save the receipts, and invest the HSA instead of spending it. Years from now, you can reimburse yourself for those old bills tax-free. For 2026, you can put in up to $4,400 with self-only coverage or $8,750 with family coverage, employer contributions included.

**Why bother:** It turns a dreaded expense into a long-term growth account. A dental bill today can become tax-free spending money in twenty years.

**Do:** Keep a simple folder of receipts (digital is fine) and invest the balance once you have a cushion.

**Don't:** Choose a high-deductible plan only for the HSA if you have heavy medical needs. A plan that bankrupts you in a bad year defeats the purpose. Also check your state's rules, since a few states tax HSAs differently.

### 3. Ask Your Employer to Pay Your Student Loans

**What it really is:** Employers can pay up to $5,250 a year toward your student loans, and it doesn't count as taxable income to you. Congress made that permanent. The catch is that most companies never advertise it. You have to ask.

**How to squeeze it:** Email HR and ask, in writing, whether the company offers student loan repayment help under its educational assistance program. If it does, find out whether payments go straight to your servicer. Switching jobs? Raise it during negotiation. It's real money that doesn't show up in the salary number.

**Why bother:** Five thousand dollars of loan principal gone is worth far more than a free pizza Friday.

**Do:** Remember the $5,250 is shared with tuition help, so you don't get both at full size.

**Don't:** Assume "no" because nobody mentioned it. Nobody mentions anything.

### 4. Use Pre-Tax Dollars for Childcare (Dependent Care FSA)

**What it really is:** A dependent care FSA lets you pay for daycare, after-school care and summer day camp with money taken out before taxes. The 2026 limit jumped to $7,500 per household.

**How to squeeze it:** Sign up during open enrollment. You generally can't change it mid-year. Add up your real childcare costs first, then elect that amount, not a dollar more. Ask a tax preparer to compare it against the child and dependent care tax credit. Lower earners sometimes do better with the credit, higher earners with the FSA.

**Why bother:** Childcare is one of the biggest bills a parent has. Paying it with pre-tax money is a built-in discount.

**Do:** Check that your employer has adopted the higher limit. They have to update their plan.

**Don't:** Over-fund it. Unspent money can be lost, depending on your plan's rules.

### 5. Buy Your Company's Stock at a Discount (ESPP)

**What it really is:** An employee stock purchase plan lets you buy shares of your employer through payroll at a discount, often up to 15%. Some plans add a "lookback," which applies the discount to the lower of two prices. That can make the discount even bigger.

**How to squeeze it:** Read your plan document for the discount, the lookback and any holding rules. Plenty of savvy people sell the shares right after buying and treat the discount as a small raise. Others hold, but only if they'd want that stock anyway.

**Why bother:** An instant gain just for participating is rare anywhere in investing.

**Do:** Keep records, because the discount gets taxed as income.

**Don't:** Put in money you'll need before the purchase date. And don't let one company be both your paycheck and your whole portfolio.

### 6. Claim the Retirement "Match" Most People Miss (Saver's Credit and Saver's Match)

**What it really is:** If your income is modest and you put money into a retirement account, the government may reward you. Right now it's the Saver's Credit, a tax credit on your savings. Starting with 2027, it becomes the Saver's Match, where the government can deposit up to $1,000 straight into your account.

**How to squeeze it:** Contribute to an IRA or workplace plan, then check the income limits at [https://www.irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov) by searching "Saver's Credit." You claim it with Form 8880. Side hustlers and part-timers qualify too, since IRA contributions count.

**Why bother:** Free money for doing something you should do anyway, built for people who aren't rich.

**Do:** Mark your calendar for the 2027 changes.

**Don't:** Expect the current credit if you owe little or no tax. It's nonrefundable. The match is designed to fix that, but the details are still being finalized, so confirm later.

### 7. Call Your Card Company and Ask for a Lower Rate

**What it really is:** Credit card interest rates aren't carved in stone. They're negotiable more often than anyone admits. In a recent survey, most people who asked got a lower rate. Only about a quarter of cardholders ever pick up the phone.

**How to squeeze it:** Call the number on the back of your card. Try this:

> "Hi, I've been a customer for a while and always pay on time. My rate is higher than offers I'm seeing elsewhere. Can you lower my APR?"

If they say no, ask about a hardship program, or whether a different card from them carries a lower rate.

**Why bother:** If you carry a balance, a few points off the rate is real money every month. It's the cheapest ten minutes you'll spend this year.

**Do:** Know your current rate and have a competing offer in your pocket.

**Don't:** Expect miracles with a damaged credit score. A strong score gets better results. And don't call when you're behind on payments and hope for the best.

### 8. Search for Money the Government Is Holding for You

**What it really is:** When a company can't find you, it hands your money to the state: old paychecks, utility deposits, forgotten accounts, insurance refunds. About one in seven people has some waiting. States return billions every year.

**How to squeeze it:** Search [https://www.missingmoney.com](https://www.missingmoney.com) and [https://unclaimed.org](https://unclaimed.org). Search every state you've lived in, plus old and maiden names. Then check [https://www.treasurydirect.gov/savings-bonds/treasury-hunt/](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/savings-bonds/treasury-hunt/) for matured savings bonds you might have forgotten.

**Why bother:** It costs nothing and takes twenty minutes. Even a small find beats most coupon hunting.

**Do:** Claim directly through the official state site.

**Don't:** Ever pay someone to "recover" your money. Official claims are free, and unsolicited emails about unclaimed money are a classic scam.

### 9. Open the Extra Roth Door (Mega Backdoor Roth, If Your Plan Allows)

**What it really is:** Some workplace plans let you add after-tax money beyond the normal limit, then convert it into Roth savings that grows tax-free. It's the most advanced item here, and only a minority of plans allow it. For 2026, total 401(k) contributions from you and your employer can reach $72,000.

**How to squeeze it:** Ask HR one precise question: "Does our plan allow after-tax contributions and in-plan Roth conversions?" If yes, find out how fast conversions happen. If no, move on without guilt.

**Why bother:** If you're already maxing the basics, it's a way to grow far more tax-free money.

**Do:** Make sure your emergency fund and employer match are handled first.

**Don't:** Force this if you're still building the basics. It's a finishing move, not an opening move.

### 10. Collect Bank Bonuses Without Getting Burned

**What it really is:** Banks pay new customers to show up. A typical bonus runs a few hundred dollars for opening an account and setting up direct deposit. It's a marketing cost, and you get to pocket it.

**How to squeeze it:** Pick offers with realistic requirements. A smaller bonus you'll actually earn beats a huge one that demands a mountain of cash. Write down the deadline, the deposit requirement and how long you must keep the account open.

**Why bother:** Do it once or twice a year and it's a nice bit of extra income for relatively little work.

**Do:** Remember bonuses are taxable interest income, so expect a tax form.

**Don't:** Close the account early, since banks can claw the bonus back. And skip any account with monthly fees that eat the reward.

## Bonus Moves Worth a Look

**529-to-Roth rollover:** Leftover college savings can move into a Roth IRA for the student, up to $35,000 over a lifetime. The account must be 15 years old, and annual limits apply.

**I bonds:** A government savings bond that follows inflation. You can buy up to $10,000 a year at [https://www.treasurydirect.gov](https://www.treasurydirect.gov), and the rate resets every May and November.

**Trump Accounts:** Children born from 2025 through 2028 may qualify for a one-time $1,000 government deposit, but you have to claim it. Look at [https://www.trumpaccounts.gov](https://www.trumpaccounts.gov) and IRS Form 4547 for the current process.

**A myth worth skipping:** You'll see "infinite banking" pitched as the thing rich people hide. It uses expensive whole life insurance as a savings tool. After fees and commissions, the returns are usually disappointing. Be skeptical of anyone selling it to you.

## The Moneymaxxing Playbook: Play It Like Chess

Chess players don't win by grabbing every piece. They win by order, patience and not losing their queen. Your money works the same way.

**The opening (this week):** Develop the easy pieces first. Audit your recent spending with my [ChatGPT Budget Analysis: How to Audit Your Last 3 Months of Expenses in 5 Minutes](/blog/chatgpt-budget-analysis). Move idle cash to where it earns (way 1). Run the unclaimed money search (way 8). Make the card company call (way 7). One evening, four wins.

**Developing your pieces (this month):** Open enrollment season is when your benefits get decided. Elect your HSA and dependent care FSA, ask about student loan help, and read your ESPP terms. These are the moves you can't easily redo mid-year.

**The middlegame (this quarter):** Check the Saver's Credit and upcoming Saver's Match, line up a bank bonus if it truly fits, and ask HR about after-tax contributions.

**The endgame (every year):** Put a yearly review on your calendar. Rates move, limits change, and benefits reset. Checking once a year beats checking never.

**Three rules so you don't lose:**

- **Protect your queen:** Your emergency fund is the piece you never sacrifice. Every fancy move comes after it.
- **Clear the high-interest debt first:** No tax trick outruns a 25% card rate.
- **Test every move with one question:** If this goes wrong, can I still pay rent? If yes, play it.

## The Real Point of Moneymaxxing

The trend will fade. The habit shouldn't. Looking at where your money sleeps, what your employer offers and what you're entitled to is a skill, and it's one you build once and use for decades.

You don't have to do all ten this month. Pick two. Do them well. Then notice how different your banking app feels when you open it with both eyes.
