I was on Reddit a couple of weeks ago and stumbled onto a subreddit where a lot of women were asking questions and swapping opinions about one specific topic. Photos, screenshots, experiences, a genuine amount of buzz. I found it intriguing enough to dig in.

The questions kept repeating in different forms. Should I get a BBL? What should my budget be if I'm getting one? How do I save up for one without touching my savings? I have the money, can anyone share how they look afterward and recommend a surgeon?

If you're a woman reading this, you probably already know what a BBL is. If you're a man, it's a Brazilian butt lift, a surgical procedure to reshape the body.

I want to give some real clarity to those first few questions, and if you're considering one yourself, this should give you an actual roadmap instead of a vague guess.

## Start with the 50-30-20 rule

There's a budgeting principle called the 50-30-20 rule, and it's a useful lens for a goal like this. Fifty percent of your income goes toward needs, the things you genuinely can't live without: rent, mortgage, groceries, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments, basic healthcare.

Thirty percent goes toward wants: restaurants, takeout, entertainment, shopping, subscriptions, non-essential upgrades, travel.

The remaining 20% goes toward financial security, emergency savings, retirement, investments, and other longer-term goals.

Here's the part that matters most. Your investment portion isn't up for negotiation for a goal like this. The only place a BBL budget should realistically come from is your wants category.

## What a BBL actually costs

A typical U.S. BBL runs approximately $8,000 to $12,000 on the lower end, with a more common range of $12,000 to $18,000. High-end surgeons in major cities charge $18,000 to $25,000 and up, and the premium tier runs $25,000 to $30,000 and beyond, usually the range celebrities are working with.

Don't assume the advertised surgical fee is the final bill either. Depending on the clinic, anesthesia, facility and operating room fees, medical tests, compression garments, medications, and aftercare often get added on top.

For the rest of this breakdown, I'll use $15,000 as a reasonable reference point towards budgeting for a BBL.

## The math, by income level

Say you're earning $5,000 a month. Under the 50-30-20 rule, that's $2,500 toward needs, $1,500 toward wants, and $1,000 toward financial goals. If you saved your entire wants budget toward a $15,000 BBL, you'd hit that number in 10 months.

Realistically though, that means sacrificing every other want for nearly a year straight, and one missed month due to indiscipline or a lack of accountability pushes the timeline out well past a year. Most people can't actually do that, since real life still needs some of that wants budget. A more sustainable approach is setting aside 15% of your wants category specifically, which brings the picture down to earth.

Here's how that plays out across different income levels, saving 15% of your wants budget monthly toward the same $15,000 target.

**$5,000 income:**
$2,500 needs, $1,500 wants, $1,000 investments. Saving $750 monthly (from $1,500) reaches $15,000 in approximately 20 months, or 1 year and 6 months.

**$4,500 income:**
$2,250 needs, $1,350 wants, $900 investments. Saving $675 monthly (from $1,350) reaches the target in approximately 22 months, or 1 year and 8 months.

**$4,000 income:**
$2,000 needs, $1,200 wants, $800 investments. Saving $600 monthly (from $1,200) reaches the target in approximately 25 months, or 2 years.

**$3,500 income:**
$1,750 needs, $1,050 wants, $700 investments. Saving $525 (from $1,050) monthly reaches the target in approximately 28 months, or 2 years and 3 months.

**$3,000 income:**
$1,500 needs, $900 wants, $600 investments. Saving $450 monthly (from $900) reaches the target in approximately 33 months, or 2 years and 8 months.

**$2,500 income:**
$1,250 needs, $750 wants, $500 investments. Saving $375 monthly (from $750) reaches the target in approximately 40 months, or roughly 3 years and 4 months.

## My honest take

Most women probably won't have the patience to wait close to two years for a BBL, since it tends to be a want people feel urgently about. If you fall in the $4,500 to $5,000 income range and you're genuinely patient, this is a doable, if long, road with real discipline behind it.

If your income falls further down that list and this is something you feel you can't wait for, I'd gently say this isn't the goal to chase right now. And if you're earning under $3,500 a month, I'll be direct: this isn't currently affordable without compromising the parts of your budget that actually protect you.

The lesson underneath all of this isn't really about BBLs specifically. If you can't afford something without touching your investments, don't do it. You don't have to do it. Not at the expense of the portion of your budget that's building your actual security.
