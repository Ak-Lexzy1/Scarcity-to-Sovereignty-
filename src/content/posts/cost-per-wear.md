I'm wearing one of my favorite pieces right now, an old hoodie I reach for whenever I'm in a relaxed mood and writing. I genuinely love this thing, and part of why I can wear it as much as I want is that I planned for it. I make sure I actually get the most out of it.

It gives me the exact comfort a casual piece should. The texture, the color, the whole feel of it just works.

That brings me to something worth understanding, which is: cost per wear.

## What cost per wear actually means

Cost per wear is simply how much value you're getting from something you own, relative to how often you actually use it. Clothes and shoes are the obvious examples, but the same math applies to almost anything you buy.

Say I bought this hoodie for $200 and I've worn it 20 times. My cost per wear is $10, which is $200 divided by 20. The more I wear it, the lower that number goes, and the more that $200 actually feels justified.

This is exactly the kind of value you want to be extracting from everything in your closet.

## How to actually track this

Spread out your closet, especially if your wardrobe is extensive. This matters more than it sounds like it should. When you can actually see what you own, you stop buying duplicates by accident.

Most people have bought something they already owned at some point, without realizing it, simply because they weren't paying attention. That's how you end up with four nearly identical shirts and no memory of how it happened.

Before your next shopping trip, spread your clothes out and ask yourself two questions. What do I already have? What do I actually need? That's how you prioritize instead of just browsing and grabbing.

Attention to detail is the whole game here, unless you've got money to burn buying the same kind of thing over and over without noticing.

## Quality is the other half of the equation

None of this works without quality. If my hoodie had been cheaply made, there's no way I'd have gotten 20 wears out of it and counting. A low cost-per-wear number only happens when the item is actually built to last that long.

Cheap and frequently replaced usually ends up costing more per wear than something well made that you keep for years. Buying fewer, better pieces tends to beat buying a lot of forgettable ones.

Get real value for your money. Know your cost per wear, and let it guide what actually earns a spot in your closet.
