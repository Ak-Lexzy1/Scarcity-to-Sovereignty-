If you're a single parent reading this, listen up, as this is likely one of the biggest opportunities you've been searching for.

There's a version of you that wanted this before life got complicated. Before the diapers, the daycare bills, the 6 a.m. wake-ups before a full day of work. Somewhere in there, "going back to school" got filed under someday, and someday has a way of never actually arriving on its own.

Here's something worth knowing before you write off the idea entirely: you're not the only one carrying this. According to the Urban Institute, nearly 1 in 5 undergraduate students nationally, over 3 million people, are currently raising children while enrolled in college. This isn't a rare, lonely path. It's a well-traveled one, and there's real financial aid for single parents built specifically around it, if you know where to actually look.

## Start with the FAFSA, even if you think you won't qualify

The Free Application for Federal Student Aid is the single most important form in this entire process, and most people skip it out of assumption rather than fact. Here's something genuinely underused: single parents get a more generous eligibility threshold than other applicants for the maximum Pell Grant. Most students need income at or below 175% of the federal poverty line to qualify for the max award. Single parents only need to be at or below 225%.

What that looks like in real numbers: a family of three in the continental US can earn up to $45,185 a year and still qualify for the maximum Pell Grant. That's higher than a lot of people assume the cutoff would be.

For the 2026-27 award year, the maximum Federal Pell Grant is $7,395, with a minimum award of $740 for full-time students. This is grant money, not a loan. You don't pay it back.

**The deadline that actually matters:** the federal FAFSA deadline for 2026-27 is June 30, 2027, but don't let that date fool you into waiting. Most state grant programs and individual colleges have their own priority deadlines that are far earlier, often within the first few months the form opens, and funding at many schools runs out on a first-come, first-served basis. File the FAFSA as early as possible, not close to any deadline.

Apply at [studentaid.gov](https://studentaid.gov)

## Scholarships built specifically for single parents

Beyond federal aid, there's a small but real world of private scholarships and grants aimed directly at single mothers and, in some cases, single fathers, returning to school. Here are three verified, currently legitimate options.

### Soroptimist Live Your Dream Awards

This is the strongest option on this list, both in scale and in timing. Since 1972, Soroptimist International of the Americas has distributed more than $47 million in education awards to tens of thousands of women through this program. In a recent year alone, the organization distributed over $2-3 million to more than 1,500 to 2,300 women nationally.

**Applications are open right now**, from August 1 through November 15 each year. Awards start around $1,000 to $1,500 at the local club level and can climb to $3,000-$5,000 at the regional level, with a total possible value of up to $16,000 across club, regional, and international judging.

**Eligibility:** women age 18 or older who provide the primary financial support for their family, and who are enrolled in, or accepted to, a vocational or skills training program or an undergraduate degree program. Many recipients have faced real hardship, poverty, domestic violence, the death of a spouse, or reentry after job loss, and the program is built specifically with that reality in mind.

Apply at [soroptimist.org](https://www.soroptimist.org/our-work/live-your-dream-awards/apply-for-the-live-your-dream-awards.html)

### Patsy Takemoto Mink Education Foundation

Named for Congresswoman Patsy Mink, the first woman of color elected to the US House of Representatives, this foundation was established in 2003 to carry forward her commitment to educational access for low-income women. Each year, it awards five Education Support Awards of up to $5,000 each, disbursed in two installments across the academic year, usable for tuition, books, childcare, or living expenses while enrolled.

**Eligibility:** women at least 17 years old, low-income by household size, mothers with minor children, pursuing a first postsecondary degree or credential at a nonprofit, accredited US institution.

**One important timing note:** the deadline is August 1 each year, which means this year's cycle has already closed as of this writing. Bookmark this one now, applications for the next cycle typically open around May.

Learn more at [patsyminkfoundation.org](https://www.patsyminkfoundation.org)

### Jeannette Rankin Women's Scholarship Fund

Active since 1976, this fund has awarded more than $2.7 million historically to over 900 women, supporting low-income women returning to education later in life. The National Scholar Grant provides up to $2,500 annually, renewable for up to five years, distributed directly to recipients as unrestricted, non-tuition funding.

**Eligibility:** women age 35 or older (or 25 or older specifically for residents of Montana, Georgia, or those attending a Tribal college), demonstrating financial need, pursuing a technical education, first associate's degree, or first bachelor's degree.

The current cycle is closed, but the next one typically opens in early November, so this timing actually lines up well if you're reading this in the fall.

Apply at [rankinfoundation.org](https://rankinfoundation.org)

## How to actually approach this

Don't treat any single one of these as "the answer." The smartest strategy is stacking: file your FAFSA first, since it opens the door to federal, state, and often school-based aid all at once, then layer private scholarships like Soroptimist on top. Most single parents who successfully fund their education aren't relying on one lucky grant. They're combining several smaller sources into something that actually covers the bill.

And if a deadline has already passed by the time you're reading this, that's not a dead end. Bookmark this page, set a calendar reminder for the next opening, and come back. These programs run every year. You can also share this post with individuals and groups who may find it helpful.
